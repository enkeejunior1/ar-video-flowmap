TL;DR

Few-step autoregressive video models do not need DMD. With two guidance terms on the teacher, pure FlowMap distillation matches DMD students on VBench with substantially higher sample diversity.

Existing few-step AR video models rely on distribution matching distillation (DMD), which reduces sample diversity and requires a large bidirectional teacher. We analyze what limits AR FlowMap distillation and add two guidance mechanisms: anti-drifting guidance corrects drifted student rollouts, and motion-aware guidance makes motion easier for the student to learn. The approach works across π-Flow, MeanFlow and consistency distillation.

Background: AR FlowMap distillation vs. DMD

A few-step AR student generates a video chunk by chunk. FlowMap distillation trains it to follow the denoising flow of the AR teacher, in two stages that differ only in where the history comes from. The previous recipe, SF-DMD, instead trains the student's rollout with a large bidirectional teacher.

Method

We analyze what limits AR FlowMap distillation and find two problems. During self-forcing, the student's rollouts drift and the AR teacher cannot correct them. Moving regions have a complex velocity field that a few-step student finds hard to learn. We address each with a guidance mechanism on the teacher, and plug the guided velocity directly into the FlowMap objective (π-Flow, MeanFlow or dCM).

Why do FlowMap students fail to learn motion, and how does the guidance fix it?

A FlowMap student follows the teacher's velocity field in a few long steps, which is easier when that field changes smoothly, that is, when its Lipschitz constant is small. After the first chunk, the history already fixes the static background, and only moving objects change from frame to frame. Their velocity field varies sharply, so the student blurs or distorts them.

Guidance can smooth the field, but in later chunks only the right kind does. In the first chunk, every guidance lowers the Lipschitz constant. In the second chunk, CFG raises it at larger strengths, since a stronger text prompt does not guide frame-to-frame motion. Skip-layer guidance does not use the prompt and lowers it, but saturates early because it also perturbs the static background. Motion-aware guidance applies the skip only where the teacher is uncertain, which is where things move, and gives the largest reduction.

Experiments

We distill the chunk-wise AR teachers of Causal-rCM and Causal Forcing (causal Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B). Every student generates each chunk in 4 NFE, without guidance at inference. VBench scores are five-seed means over 946 prompts. Diversity is the mean pairwise feature distance among the five videos of a prompt.

Each guidance term fixes one failure of FlowMap distillation: anti-drifting guidance stops the 30 s drift, and motion-aware guidance stops the motion breakdown.

Main results

5 s video generation

On 5 s videos, our π-Flow students match the DMD students, which are distilled from 14B teachers, on VBench (84.87 and 84.76) without any DMD stage. They also have the highest Quality scores and are more diverse across seeds.

Long video generation

On 30 s videos with the Causal-rCM teacher, all three of our students lose less imaging quality over 30 s than the DMD student, nearly halving its drop (ΔIQ). The 30 s videos are at the top of the page.

Ablation study

★Anti-drifting guidance does not hurt diversity

Unlike the reverse KL of DMD on \(x_0\), anti-drifting guidance does not reduce diversity in practice: with it, the student is more diverse across seeds and also scores higher on VBench.

Motion-aware guidance

Motion-aware guidance scores higher than skip-layer guidance with any fixed weight, including the weight that matches its mean.

Anti-drifting guidance

Without anti-drifting guidance, both students drift more over 30 s (ΔIQ and ΔAQ) and score lower on 5 s VBench.

Motion-aware guidance + anti-drifting guidance

Each term alone raises VBench Total by more than 2.5 points over CFG only; with both, the student matches the DMD student.
